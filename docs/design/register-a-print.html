Coalition a group of parties that contests and governs together

You vote for one candidate in one Seat. What decides who governs is how many Seats a Coalition holds once every Seat has been counted.

Why the number is 112

There are 222 Seats in the Dewan Rakyat, the elected house of Malaysia’s Parliament — one for each parliamentary constituency, each won by a single candidate. A Coalition needs 112 of them, more than half, to hold a Majority and form a government on its own.

Almost every candidate stands for a party, and almost every party stands inside a Coalition. So the Seat-by-Seat contest resolves into a much shorter question: which Coalitions, added together, get past 112.

The five Coalitions — GE16

Three contest nationwide; two contest only in Sabah and Sarawak, which hold 56 of the 222 Seats between them.

Four of the five govern together

No Coalition reached 112 at GE15 in 2022. The four now in government arrived there by agreement after polling day, not by a result on it — which is why the side that governs is a group of four Coalitions rather than a single winner, and why this site never calls any of them the winner of GE15.

Government Coalition
PH, BN, GPS and GRS together, plus minor parties — the bloc holding the Majority as of the most recent Dewan Rakyat count. Used as one term throughout this site, because it is what the arithmetic is actually about.
Non-government
Every Seat and Coalition outside that bloc. Not “the opposition”: PN is the opposition, but WARISAN, KDM, PBM and independents have declared no alignment either way, and calling them opposition would assert one for them.
