Telegram posts — three Return Trigger shapes

Three prototype posts for the proposed channel, one per shape a Return Trigger can take, so the caption voice and the choice of image can be compared and one picked, or a synthesis asked for. A and B are the same Seat Call card under two captions of about the same length that do different work; C is a trigger with no Seat to point at, and is not really an alternative to either — it is the format the aggregate triggers need whichever caption wins. B is the suggested one, for the reason set out above it.

Everything here is a placeholder: the figures, the seat, the dates, the view counts and the subscriber count are all invented and stamped SAMPLE, because the real Baseline lives in the database, not this checkout. No channel exists, nothing posts anywhere, and none of this is wired to the pipeline — this is exploration only, the same as its sibling file seat-call-card-samples.html. The Telegram chrome around each post is a rough approximation drawn by hand so the caption can be judged in something feed-shaped; only the image inside each bubble uses the site’s own tokens.

Post A — minimal, seat-anchored

The card carries the post and the caption gets out of its way: one sentence naming what changed, then the link. This is the version that survives being forwarded — what lands in someone else’s chat is the image, and the card already says the seat, the margin, the too-close dot and the arithmetic caveat, so nothing load-bearing is stranded in caption text that a forward may drop.

The tradeoff is that it explains the change but not the arrival. A reader who opted into a channel that promises to stay quiet gets no answer to “why now?”, and a one-line caption that only ever reports movement can start to feel like a ticker — the exact habit the Return Trigger rule exists to avoid. It also leans hard on the card being read at thumbnail size; the seat name and the margin do survive that, the footer caveat mostly doesn’t.

Post B — narrative, seat-anchored

Same image, but the caption names the Return Trigger out loud before it reports anything: a Seat Call changed, and that is the kind of thing this channel exists to say. It answers “why did this arrive?” in the first four words, which is the promise a quiet channel is actually making — every post that opens this way is also a reminder that silence is the default, not neglect. That is the one job a caption can do that the card cannot, and it is why this is the suggested one of the three.

It is drawn at A’s length on purpose. A first pass ran to three paragraphs — trigger, then the figure, then the arithmetic framing as its own closing sentence — and read well on this page and badly in a feed: Telegram folds a long caption under a photo behind a Show more, and the paragraph it hides is the last one, which is exactly the caveat that must not be the hidden part. So the framing is folded into the same sentence as the figure rather than trailing after it: arithmetic against the 2022 result, not calibrated, and inside the too-close band. Two lines, nothing below the fold, and A’s forwarding discipline is kept — everything load-bearing is still on the card as well.

The residual tradeoff is voice, not length: opening every post by naming its own trigger is a house tic, and it will get repetitive faster than a bare card would. Worth revisiting after a dozen real posts rather than deciding now.

Post C — aggregate, no Seat to point at

An Election Status change has no Seat to illustrate, so the image has to be its own thing. Rather than invent a hero number, this card draws the state the status is actually in: three stops — dissolution, nomination, polling — with the first filled and the other two hollow and marked not set. That is the honest shape of “called” in Malaysia, where dissolution starts the election and the Election Commission announces polling afterwards, and it turns the one thing nobody knows yet into the picture instead of a caveat under it.

Underneath, a single thin bar puts the Projection where it stands with the 112 line marked, so a reader arriving on the biggest news day this site will ever have gets the whole-chamber answer without a Seat-level claim attached. The Government Coalition side is drawn in neutral ink, not one coalition’s colour, because that block is four coalitions. The same card, with the timeline swapped for a state result, is what a State Election Signal post would use.

The tradeoff: it is a second card format to build and keep honest, and it is the post most likely to be screenshotted out of context — hence “nothing about the Projection changed today” sitting in the caption and the framing line sitting on the card.
