What a Coalition is, and why there are five

A Coalition is a group of parties that contests and governs together. It is the first word this site assumes you already know — so here it is.

In short One vote, one Seat, one candidate. Everything else is addition.

You vote once. The result is a sum.

On polling day you mark one candidate, in one Seat — the parliamentary constituency you live in. There are 222 of them, and each one is won outright by whoever gets the most votes there.

Almost every candidate stands for a party, and almost every party stands inside a Coalition. So once all 222 Seats are counted, the question stops being about candidates and becomes a much shorter one: which Coalitions, added together, get past 112.

The number 112 is simply more than half of 222. Nothing about it is a rule of thumb.

112 is the whole game.

A Coalition holding 112 Seats or more has a Majority — enough to form a government on its own, without needing anybody else to agree. Below 112, it needs somebody else.

Where they stand Sabah and Sarawak hold 56 of the 222 Seats between them — which is why two of the five contest nowhere else.

Three contest everywhere. Two contest one state each.

Pakatan Harapan (PH), Barisan Nasional (BN) and Perikatan Nasional (PN) put up candidates across the whole country.

Gabungan Parti Sarawak (GPS) contests only in Sarawak, and Gabungan Rakyat Sabah (GRS) only in Sabah. Both sit in the Government Coalition; PN does not.

Two words to keep This site uses both of these exactly, everywhere, including in the Projection you came here from.
Government Coalition
PH, BN, GPS and GRS together, plus minor parties — the bloc holding the Majority as of the most recent Dewan Rakyat count.
Non-government
Every Seat and Coalition outside that bloc. Not “the opposition”: PN is the opposition, but WARISAN, KDM, PBM and independents have declared no alignment either way, and calling them opposition would assert one on their behalf.
